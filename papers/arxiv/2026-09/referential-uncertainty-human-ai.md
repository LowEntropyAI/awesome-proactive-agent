# Referential Uncertainty in Human--AI Collaboration

## Why It Matters

This study shows that hidden uncertainty is not enough: a human collaborator may accept a wrong action unless the agent accurately communicates which reference is uncertain and why.

## Proactivity Signal

Before placing a piece, the AI worker can ask for clarification or externalize uncertainty with a targeted hedge so the human helper can correct the reference.

## Evaluation Setup

Across three frontier vision-language models, separately elicited belief distributions are better calibrated than action-token probabilities, yet models ask clarification on only 3.5--16.7% of turns. In a 210-participant study, default messages lead users to accept 78% of wrong placements, while precise descriptions and targeted hedges reduce wrong-move acceptance to 36%.

## Key Limitations

The task is a controlled collaborative puzzle and the useful hedge depends on an uncertainty signal that itself misses some competing-referent cases. Poorly targeted uncertainty communication can make decisions worse.

## Use For

Use this for referential clarification, uncertainty communication, human oversight of embodied actions, calibration, and distinguishing internal uncertainty from actionable user-facing signals.
