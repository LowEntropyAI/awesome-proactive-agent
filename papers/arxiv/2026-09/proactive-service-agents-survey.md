# Proactive Service Agents: A Unified Decision Framework, Methods, and Evaluation

## Why It Matters

This survey gives proactive service agents an operational definition centered on initiative rather than long-term memory or generic autonomy. It unifies timing, content, delivery, authorization, and intervention cost within one decision problem, making it a useful map of the rapidly fragmenting field.

## Proactivity Signal

The agent infers service opportunities from incomplete user and environmental signals, then chooses among remaining silent, asking, assisting, and acting. Waiting has option value, questions have decision value, and actions are constrained by authorization, recoverability, interruption cost, and privacy risk.

## Evaluation Setup

As a survey, the paper does not introduce a new trained agent or empirical benchmark. It normalizes decision units and evidence descriptors across streaming dialogue, screen, video, software engineering, and human-agent collaboration, and organizes metrics for triggering, timing, calibration, user burden, safety, and policy value.

## Key Limitations

The unified formulation is conceptual and has not yet been validated through a common benchmark or deployment study. Because the paper is a newly released preprint, its taxonomy and coverage may change as the literature and venue status evolve.

## Use For

Use this for defining proactive service, separating proactivity from generic agency or memory, structuring ask/assist/act/silence policies, and designing evaluations that include authorization, interruption cost, and counterfactual value.
