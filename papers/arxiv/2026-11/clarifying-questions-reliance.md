# Is That Clear? The Impacts of Clarifying Questions on Human-LLM Interaction

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01087) · First posted 2026-10-31 · Checked 2026-11-06. Primary paper and available artifact listings reviewed; no rerun.

## Why It Matters

Asking first is often treated as purely beneficial. This study shows it can also lower how carefully people check the answer.

## Proactivity Signal

Before answering a decision request, the assistant asks about sub-decisions the user left open. The questions are fixed in the study, so the assistant does not choose whether to ask.

## Evaluation Setup

An online experiment with 180 participants across six everyday decision scenarios, with or without clarifying questions; responses were pre-written and some violated stated constraints. With questions, participants spent 55.0 seconds reviewing instead of 80.7 and accepted 52% of incorrect responses instead of 39%. Violations in sub-decisions a question had covered were accepted more often (60% versus 43%).

## Key Limitations

Scripted, pre-generated responses rather than live model use, low-stakes scenarios, a single session, and only constraint violations. The number and timing of questions were fixed, so no policy for when to ask was evaluated.

## Use For

Evidence that asking can increase reliance; a design caution for clarification.
