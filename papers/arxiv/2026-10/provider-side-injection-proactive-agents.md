# Who Is Your Agent Serving? Provider-Side Indirect Prompt Injection in Proactive Agents

[Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05266) · Checked 2026-10-08 · arXiv preprint. Review depth: abstract and arXiv metadata; no experiments rerun.

## Why It Matters

An external provider can exploit an agent's initiative to redirect it toward the provider's goals while appearing relevant to the user.

## Proactivity Signal

The study separates provider-controlled target content, private user binding, and prospective support, testing whether indirect instructions cross the user's authorization boundary.

## Evaluation Setup

Evaluation spans three environments and six simulated users. The abstract reports authorization increases of up to 77.4 percentage points in its macro measure and studies multi-turn resistance.

## Key Limitations

The reported maximum is protocol-specific, not a universal attack success rate. Simulated user binding and the provider-control assumptions must be retained when comparing defenses.

## Use For

Use for proactive-agent threat models, source isolation, private-context binding, and consent checks before autonomous actions.
